## C1: P-EAGLE serving report and paper

Primary capture: https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-03-13-p-eagle, displayed March 13, 2026, attributed to Amazon and NVIDIA Team (acknowledgements separately list AWS/NVIDIA contributors). It reports vLLM integration starting v0.16.0 via PR 32887, model-specific parallel-trained heads for GPT-OSS 120B/20B and Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B-Instruct, and the `parallel_drafting` configuration. These are claims in the later-captured page, not independent exact-date release attestations.

Method: a first position with actual target hidden state/token context and later masked positions with learned shared hidden-state and mask-token placeholders let one drafter forward produce K proposals. Target-model verification is still necessary; this does not turn the served autoregressive target into an unverified parallel-output model. Capacity/training adaptations matter: 4-layer drafter in the blog benchmark; it is trained to predict up to 10 tokens, while tested K is 3/5/7. A parallel-capable head is necessary. The source's introductory 'single configuration change' must not erase this precondition.

Exact main report comparison: GPT-OSS-20B on one NVIDIA B200, vLLM, MT-Bench, HumanEval, SPEED-Bench Code; concurrency C=1,2,4,8,16,32,64; both methods use linear drafting. Each method's best TPS is selected over K=3/5/7 for each serving condition. The comparison is with a publicly available vanilla EAGLE-3 checkpoint, not ordinary non-speculative decoding and not DFlash. At C=1, P/E TPS ratios are 1.55, 1.55, 1.69 respectively; at C=64 they are 1.05, 1.23, 1.25. The 1.69 maximum must remain attached to this comparator and condition. Typical account can give ranges instead of every value, but must make diminishing/load-specific gains visible. Better acceptance length also contributes, so this is not an isolation of work order with all capacity/training otherwise controlled. Throughput improvement is not a demonstrated proportional reduction of per-request latency, total operating expense, training cost, or lifecycle work.

Deployment blocker with direct reader impact: the captured report explicitly says GPT-OSS-20B with EAGLE drafters requires a one-line vLLM patch PR 36684, expected in a future release. Its historical applicability is unresolved, but omission while claiming turnkey deployment from this corpus is misleading. The benchmark also sets FP8 KV cache, async scheduling, specific large batching/CUDA-graph settings, and disables prefix caching and chunked prefill. A publication need not dump commands but should not generalize to untested hardware/settings/models or erase configuration/patch/rebenchmark work. Integrated framework support is not proof of every supported target's same speed gain.

Supporting paper: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01469v1, header 01 Feb 2026; Mude Hui (UCSC; internship AWS) and AWS coauthors. Distinct from March serving report. Paper section 5/Table 10 measures total output TPS on one H200 at C=2,4 over GPT-OSS 120B/20B/Qwen3-Coder 30B, K=3/5/7, chain drafting and an optimal-K autoregressive EAGLE-3 baseline. The paper's own baseline training includes HCA; don't silently equate this to the blog's public checkpoint. Its abstract 1.10–1.36 range is not the exact minimum over all table cells. Concrete adverse cells include Qwen30B/HumanEval K=3: Table 10 gives 0.94 at C=2 and 0.92 at C=4; section 5.3 prose gives 0.98 instead of 0.94 for C=2, an internal discrepancy. Also 120B/MT at K=7,C=4 gives 0.98. These make 'always faster' or 'all depths cost-free' indefensible. If an exact adverse number is used, bind it to the table and note the discrepancy or avoid the disputed cell; do not average it away.

Training claims have scope: section 5.1 uses 8 H200, 8192 max sequence length, Ktrain=8, COD retention 0.8, target-specific data including UltraChat/GSM8K train/OpenCodeInstruct, and more capacity/learned embeddings. Section 3 develops amortized masks plus within-sequence partitioning for memory pressure; its table's epoch-time comparison is one setup, not full total-cost parity. ParallelSpec was locally reimplemented with unavailable details and PARD adapted to EAGLE; their reported OOM/scalability results cannot establish that all rivals fail long-context training. No complete end-to-end training/serving lifecycle expense is supplied.

## C2: DFlash — substantive omission/comparison counterexample

https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036v1, header 05 Feb 2026; Jian Chen, Yesheng Liang, Zhijian Liu, UC San Diego. Pre-window research can legitimately be background or HOLD as a separate weekly item, but substantive comparison should not disappear simply because the performance is author-reported: C1 is also author/project-reported. It directly addresses parallel speculation and includes optimized serving experiments, so dismissal as only a theoretical or Transformers-only result is false. No v2 evidence was inspected or permitted.

Distinct mechanism: lightweight block diffusion drafter denoises masked positions in one forward, conditioned on fused target hidden features injected into each draft layer's KV projections/cache. The autoregressive target verifies; 'lossless' describes speculative verification preserving target behavior under the method's assumptions, not a stronger base model or proof of all implementation/sample equivalence. Training uses target-generated responses/hidden states, typically five draft layers (eight for Qwen Coder), frozen shared embedding/LM head, block size 16 (10 for LLaMA3.1), and target feature extraction. The adapter/checkpoints are trained, not automatically obtained by toggling a vLLM flag. Appendix A.1 describes 6 epochs, max sequence lengths 3072/4096, online recomputation or offline cached hidden states. These have data/compute/storage costs that no headline speed multiplier resolves.

Separate performance surfaces:
- Abstract says over 6x over autoregressive baseline and up to 2.5x relative to EAGLE-3. Table 1/section 5.1 uses Qwen3-4B/8B thinking disabled, Transformers backend, H200 default, 2048 generated-token maximum, temperatures 0 and 1, EAGLE trees 16/60 vs DFlash block16. Qwen3-8B Math500 greedy DFlash is 6.08x over AR; MT-Bench is 2.75x, not a uniform 6x. Do not compare 6x directly with P-EAGLE's 1.69x. Section 5.1 prose's claimed '4.9x corresponding to 2.4x over EAGLE-3 (16)' does not line up with table averages around 1.8x for tree16; avoid converting this loosely stated ratio into a precise new derived claim.
- Section 5.3/Table 3 is a strong non-headline counterexample: SGLang FA4, one B200, Spec-v2 overlap, C=1/4/8/16/32, Qwen3-4B/8B/Coder30B. Up to 5.1x is over non-speculative AR on Qwen3-8B Math500 at C=1; at C=32 that case is 2.8x. This table does not compare EAGLE-3 and does not rank P-EAGLE.
- Section 5.4.1/Table 4 directly compares EAGLE-3 on LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct: same UltraChat/ShareGPT training data, official EAGLE checkpoint, single B200 SGLang Flashinfer, Spec-v1 without overlap because Spec-v2 lacks EAGLE tree support, tree10/60 versus block10, C=1–32. For HumanEval C=1, DFlash 2.8x over AR versus EAGLE 2.0x; C=32 DFlash 1.8x versus tree10 1.2x/tree60 0.6x. This is important evidence against 'DFlash only has an incomparable abstract' even though P-EAGLE ranking remains unsupported. A concise account can mention the controlled SGLang comparison without reproducing all numbers.

Limits: DFlash's tested backends/models/training/concurrency differ from P-EAGLE; absence of a matched P-EAGLE-vs-DFlash test means no rank or adoption winner. Both changing architecture and training can contribute. Layer count, block size, target feature count and batch/verification cost affect speed; increasing capacity/acceptance length does not guarantee faster serving. Dynamic block scheduling and more advanced deployment generalizations remain future/unestablished in this source. Paper code/model links do not independently prove usable historical integration/version availability or successful replication.
