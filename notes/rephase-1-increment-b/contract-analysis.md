## Semantic versus mechanical identity

Semantic identity is the canonical, schema-valid reader object: all reader-visible issue/window metadata, resolved fixed labels, cover/frontmatter, ordered result prose, final summary, citation placement/keys/order, and exact ordered bibliography fields. Its persisted bytes remain the actual review target. Changing any of these values requires a new Reader-Surface review. Canonical serialization should be deterministic so key ordering/format churn is not introduced as ordinary operation.

Mechanical identity contains checkpoint-accepted refs and exact authored replay refs, current implementation/contract, safe provenance digests, helper/style closure and exact generated main/bib/style bytes. Authored replay refs establish identity and reproducibility, not upstream acceptance. A runner timestamp, internal annotation, rationale, or other nonreader upstream change must not change semantic identity. It may require rebuilt mechanical evidence if it affects safe comments or source bytes. Any actual source/support/PDF byte change still invalidates the applicable exact-source, visual and Human approval evidence; semantic-input equality is not permission to reuse those approvals.

The required comparison is two independent equalities:

1. recomputed canonical reader object from accepted checkpoint authority plus exact, constrained publication-authored choices equals the exact reviewed object;
2. deterministic render of that object under the bound source closure equals exact `main.tex`, `references.bib`, and copied style bytes.

Fresh hashes of independently mutable input/output files do not prove either equality.
